# A small guide to rock pools

When the sea goes out, it leaves pieces of itself behind. In the hollows of a
rocky shore, a few buckets of seawater wait for the tide to come back. Each
one is a rock pool, and each one is busy: anemones close up like wet sweets,
periwinkles graze the weed, and a shrimp you did not see a moment ago is
suddenly there, clear as glass.

This guide is for anyone going to look for the first time, on their own or
with children. It is short on purpose. Read it on the way to the beach.

- [When to go](when-to-go.md): the tide, and what to take.
- [At a glance](at-a-glance.md): the whole guide in five lines.
- [Words you will meet](glossary.md): the names on the signs and in the books.
- [Field notes](field-notes.md): what I saw on one morning in March.

The rule that matters most comes first: everything you find, you put back as
you found it. The pool was there before you, and it should be there after.
