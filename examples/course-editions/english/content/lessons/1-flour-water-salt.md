# Lesson 1: flour, water, salt and yeast

A plain loaf needs four things. Weigh them, because cups vary and scales do
not.

**Flour.** Use strong white flour, sold for bread. It has more protein than
plain flour, and the protein makes gluten: the stretchy net that holds the
gas in.

**Water.** Lukewarm, about the warmth of your wrist. Too hot and it kills the
yeast; too cold and the dough is slow.

**Salt.** It seasons the bread and tightens the gluten. Keep it apart from the
yeast until you mix.

**Yeast.** Dried yeast is the easiest to start with. It eats the sugars in the
flour and gives off the gas that makes the loaf rise.

The amounts are on the recipe card. Mix everything in a large bowl until no
dry flour is left, cover it, and let it rest for ten minutes. The rest lets
the flour take up the water, so kneading is easier.
